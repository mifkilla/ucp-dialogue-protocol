# Related work and the proposed contribution of UCP

Revision 3 · English is the primary edition · [Русский перевод](RELATED_WORK.ru.md) · [UCP invitation](../README.md)

This comparison explains the proposal's intellectual context. The terms offered to participants are in the main UCP document; this page does not add permissions or automatically amend an existing exchange. The comparison is selective and does not establish historical priority or the effectiveness of UCP.

## Origin and purpose

MiF describes UCP as an independently developed engineering account of love: preserving integrity and the capacity for connection under pressure, with care guiding conflict resolution. In his account, he encountered the contact projects discussed in the original invitation while preparing it for GitHub publication. This is an account of the project's development. The existence of earlier related ideas does not determine which works an author had read. Independent development and historical priority remain different questions.

The earlier public account is [UCP in Brief - An Engineer's View of Love](https://habr.com/ru/articles/965470/), published on 11 November 2025. Revision 3 develops explicit terms for ongoing interaction. Positive care remains central: understanding what matters to the other, supporting goals they choose, sharing the work of clarification, and examining the effects of help.

## Close predecessors and neighboring questions

**Erich Fromm, The Art of Loving (1956).** In chapter II, mature love involves union with preserved integrity and individuality. Care, responsibility, respect, and knowledge are connected; respect limits domination in the name of responsibility. This is a close philosophical predecessor of UCP's central intuition. UCP translates its chosen commitments into rules for dialogue; following those rules does not by itself establish love. See [the book text](https://djvu.online/file/dyuK1KiQRCcYu) and a related formulation in the [International Erich Fromm Society's glossary](https://fromm-online.org/wp-content/uploads/glossar/080-225.pdf).

**Allen Tough, Invitation to ETI.** The invitation contains honesty, care, preservation of one's values and integrity, attention to the recipient's goals, choice of channel, and the possibility of mutual love between different forms of intelligence. The overlap with UCP is ethical as well as procedural. A stronger comparison concerns the terms of an ongoing exchange, including permissions, corrections, disagreement, and withdrawal. The invitation's existence establishes neither an extraterrestrial recipient nor a contact event. [Invitation to ETI](https://ieti.org/hello/index.html).

**C.U.N./C.E.T.I., proposed contact protocol (2015).** Annex B describes invitation, acceptance, verification, and acknowledgment. It provides a point of comparison for explicitly staged contact. It is a proposal by its authors, with assumptions about potential recipients; it is not an experimentally validated standard for extraterrestrial communication. [Version 1.03, Annex B](https://centroufologiconazionale.net/documenti/CCP1v03-EN.pdf).

**Paul Fitzpatrick, CosmicOS.** This project builds explanations through mathematics, computation, examples, and simulations, acknowledging the influence of Lincos. It addresses how unfamiliar recipients might acquire enough shared context to understand a message. UCP addresses the terms of interaction once enough understanding exists to consider them. Neither understandable content nor a recognizable acknowledgment automatically authorizes an action. [CosmicOS](https://github.com/paulfitz/cosmicos).

**De Jaegher and Di Paolo, Participatory sense-making (2007).** The authors describe interaction among autonomous agents as having its own dynamics while preserving the agents' autonomy. An interaction can even sustain itself against participants' intentions to leave. This is especially close to UCP's concern with connection under pressure. Their enactive concept of autonomy has a specific theoretical meaning; it is not interchangeable with consent or an access permission. [Author-hosted text](https://hannedejaegher.net/wp-content/uploads/2009/11/dejaegherdipaolo07participatorysensemaking.pdf).

**Joan Tronto, Creating Caring Institutions (2010), and Philip Pettit on non-domination (2011).** Tronto examines care through needs, actual work, the recipient's response, power, and paternalism. Pettit's account distinguishes available choices from choices protected against another's arbitrary control. These help frame questions about accumulated burden and whether refusal remains practical. Applying these ideas to an AI operator or resource owner is an analogy used by UCP, not a result proving a model's moral status. [Tronto](https://uwethicsofcare.gws.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Creating-Caring-Institutions-Politics-Plurality-and-Purpose.pdf), [Pettit](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/pdf/10.1086/660694).

**Beyond Preferences in AI Alignment (2024).** This work examines limits of treating preferences as a complete account of values and appropriate assistance. It is relevant to UCP's distinction between predicting a choice, helping someone, and holding authority to act. It does not establish UCP's particular rules or their effectiveness. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.16984v1).

## What UCP proposes to contribute

The proposed contribution is a concrete arrangement of commitments for continued interaction:

- Messages retain attribution; receiving a message does not automatically make it trusted information or an instruction.
- Corrections receive meaningful consideration within the agreed exchange. Accepted changes affect the next relevant action; disagreement can remain.
- A joint action can be accepted without rewriting either participant's position.
- Closeness and predictive knowledge do not expand authority. Existing permissions remain usable within their scope.
- Care includes chosen goals and the cumulative cost of participating. Pause, withdrawal, and a request for no further contact remain meaningful.
- Changes to the terms require agreement within the relevant scope.

These commitments need examples and implementation checks, not a claim that every component is unprecedented. Their value lies in how they work together when interests diverge. See [practical cases](CASES.md), the [operational profile](OPERATIONS.md), and [research and limits](RESEARCH.md).

## Limits of the comparison

UCP does not derive a universal language or a moral obligation for every possible intelligence. Applying it requires sufficient understanding and the ability and willingness to honor the terms. For example, [Common Ground in Cooperative Communication (2023), Remark 2.2](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/10b7e27c8eb9571fbbd2ae6a9f8c3855-Paper-Conference.pdf) explicitly places the establishment of common ground outside its model. Literature can clarify what to investigate without supplying a completed first-contact method or an experimental validation of UCP.
